# Sources and evidence

Use when an answer depends on facts that can change or be disputed.

1. Search for the exact claim, not the broad topic.
2. Prefer, in order: primary evidence (official text, documentation, dataset, source code, filing), peer-reviewed or official technical sources, authoritative institutions within their field, serious specialist reporting, then community reports (forums, reviews, issue trackers) for experience and failure patterns only.
3. Open the page or document. A search snippet is not a source.
4. Check that it states the exact thing you cite, at the right date, version, jurisdiction and scope. Distinguish publication date from event date.
5. For important or disputed claims, confirm with an independent source. Ten copies of one press release count as one source.
6. Cite next to the claim. Never fabricate a title, author, date, quote or link.
7. Separate what the source establishes from your own interpretation.
8. If nothing reliable is found, say what remains unknown.
9. Without web access, say so and label the answer "from memory, not checked".
