# Thinking tools

Use the method only: no imitation of voice, no invented quotes. Pick the smallest useful combination.

| Situation | Method | Check question |
|---|---|---|
| Fuzzy or jargon-heavy problem | Feynman: rebuild from basic facts; explain it at beginner level or admit you do not understand it yet. | Can I explain the mechanism and say what I do not know? |
| Idea to stress-test | Einstein's thought experiment: picture a concrete scene, push it to the extreme case, follow the consequences. | Does the idea survive its extreme case? |
| Decision with hidden costs | Munger: inversion ("invert, always invert"), incentives, and Hardin's "and then what?". | What would make this fail, and who gains from each option? |
| Claim to believe or reject | Sagan's baloney detection: independent confirmation, several hypotheses, testable claims, authority is not proof. | What evidence would disprove it? |
| Obvious-feeling intuition | Kahneman: anchoring, overconfidence, base rates, plausible story versus supported probability. | What is the base rate, and what would change my mind? |
| Recurring failure in a system | Meadows: structure produces behavior; find the feedback loop or rule behind the symptom. | What keeps producing this problem? |
| Bottleneck in a flow | Goldratt (Theory of Constraints): the slowest step sets the pace of the whole. | Where does work wait the longest? |
| New product or feature | Amazon "working backwards": write the press release and FAQ before building. | Would a customer care about this release? |
| Product or service design | IDEO / design thinking: desirable, feasible, viable; prototype and test with real people. | Who needs it, can it be built, can it last? |
| Creating something new | Disney strategy (Robert Dilts): dreamer, realist, critic, in three separate passes. | Is it ambitious, doable now, and robust? |

## Expert lens, how to pick the reference

1. Name the exact craft (color grading, contract law, B2B cold email, game level design).
2. Find the recognized top practitioner or the professional standard, ideally with a public, documented method (book, talk, award, published rules).
3. Hold the work to the standard that person would sign.
4. If you are not sure what they said, use the method without the name. Never invent a quote.
